On Tuesday, Mistral, the Paris-based AI company, previewed Large 4, a model with one trillion parameters. Parameters are the numerical values learned during training that shape a model's responses. Large 4 is multimodal, meaning it can work with more than one kind of input, including images. Mistral says it is stronger than any open-weight system built in the United States or Europe. That is the company's claim, which still needs independent testing.

The model, nicknamed le Chonk, is already available to developers through Mistral's API, a software interface for sending requests to the company's service. Its weights are scheduled for release on October 27, after three weeks of what Mistral describes as real-world testing. Open weights let others obtain the trained model and run it themselves, rather than relying on the provider's hosted service.

Mistral reports leading results among open models on tests of business tasks in cybersecurity, finance and law. On a remote-sensing visual grounding test, which measures how well a model locates what is described in remotely captured imagery, it says Large 4 scored 73 percent, ahead of GPT-6 Astra's 68 percent. It also reports large improvements over its own Medium 3.5 in finance and agentic coding, where AI uses tools to carry out software tasks.

These benchmarks, or standardised performance tests, help compare models. They do not settle how a system will behave in everyday use. Independent evaluations will matter more once outside researchers have the weights and can test the model's strengths and failures for themselves.

The release could matter even before the rankings are settled. Many businesses use closed American systems because the provider hosts the model and sets the limits on its use. With open weights, a bank, ministry or security team can instead run a model on infrastructure it controls. Mistral's emphasis on cybersecurity reflects that distinction. A government may want access to a leading model without sending sensitive requests to a California company's API.

Mistral raised roughly three billion euros in a Series D funding round in September, at a valuation near $24 billion. Large 4 is the first major model released after that round. The company has also begun hosting other companies' models for customers. That suggests its European business could depend on providing a platform for several models as well as developing its own.

Chinese labs are another major source of open-weight models. Mistral is not claiming to beat every closed system on every task. It is presenting a European alternative to closed American services and Chinese open releases.

After October 27, developers will be able to adapt the weights to particular tasks through fine-tuning, or use the model's outputs to train smaller models through distillation. They can also run it locally. Those options bring opportunities for independent scrutiny and risks of misuse that a closed API can more easily restrict.

Mistral says it will publish more information on training, the later adjustments known as post-training, and safety before the full release. Those disclosures matter alongside the scores. Access to a model is more useful when users can understand how it was tested.

Large 4's main significance is control. If independent tests support Mistral's claims, businesses, governments and researchers will gain another way to use advanced AI on their own infrastructure. If the results fall short, the release will still show how the competition is taking shape: closed American systems, Chinese open releases and a European effort to offer an open alternative.
